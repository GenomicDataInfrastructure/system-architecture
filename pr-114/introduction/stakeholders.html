1.3 Stakeholders

In short
  • The main stakeholders are the actors of the 1+MG Data Governance, from the Genome EDIC Assembly of Members to Users and data subjects. Each expects something different from the architecture, because each has different duties. Others, such as the European Commission, auditors and the GDI project, are affected too.
  • This documentation has six kinds of readers: policy makers, legal experts, ELSI specialists, security advisors, data protection officers and implementers. Each has a reader guide.
  • The Genome EDIC Assembly of Members decides on the principles, policies and minimum requirements. Which body adopts this architecture as a whole is not decided yet.

The stakeholders and what they expect​

The actors and their names come from the governance definitions DG III. This table covers what each one expects from the architecture; the next section covers what readers need from its documentation. The expectations follow from each actor's duties in the governance. They are still to be confirmed with the stakeholders themselves, in the reviews of each page. The last column links to the pages that answer them; many of those pages are still being written. 3.3 says which scope each actor belongs to, and 3.1 what each exchanges with the system.

How to reach these groups: in the European scope, through the Genome EDIC CC, the one-stop shop for Users. In each country, through its 1+MG NCP, the main contact for the Genome EDIC and for national stakeholders DG I. To reach the taskforce that writes this documentation, open an issue in its GitHub repository.

Other stakeholders are not among the actors that the governance defines, but the architecture affects them or depends on them:

  • the European Commission, which runs HealthData@EU under the European Health Data Space (EHDS) [EHDS];
  • the data protection authorities;
  • health data access bodies (HDABs), whose data can be brought into 1+MG SPEs, and which can use 1+MG SPEs themselves DG VIII.3.2 DG VIII.3.3;
  • patient and citizen organisations, which can speak for data subjects when their expectations are confirmed;
  • auditors and certification bodies, which check the platforms and SPEs against the common requirements DG V.1.2 DG V.1.3;
  • the teams that build the interfaces to the system: for HealthData@EU, for health data access bodies, and for national systems;
  • the Genomic Data Infrastructure (GDI) project, which builds the reference implementation [GDI Starter Kit]. Until GDI ends, the system gives access only to externally governed datasets, such as Genome of Europe data [GDI D3.4]; the other types follow once the Genome EDIC is operational (ADR-0002, proposed);
  • the 1+MG working groups and the Genome EDIC committees, which define the data and metadata models and the accreditation of IT infrastructure providers DG II.1 DG III;
  • the architecture taskforce, which writes and maintains this documentation.

Some of them, such as the data protection authorities and the HDABs, also exchange information with the system: 3.1 Business context shows what.

Who decides on the architecture​

  • The Genome EDIC Assembly of Members decides on the principles and sets the minimum requirements DG I. It adopts the policies DG V.1.1, and the data models and technical and organisational measures DG VI.1.1. The Genome EDIC CC prepares its decisions, with advice from committees and advisory boards DG V.1.2.
  • Committees and working groups turn the principles into implementation rules and technical requirements DG II.1. For example, the Genome EDIC CC coordinates the definition of data models and ontologies through the Genome EDIC committees DG VI.1.2.
  • Each Member Country decides how it implements the system nationally, within the common requirements DG II.2. It records its choices in its national implementation profile.
  • This documentation is a working draft of the Genome EDIC system architecture taskforce, not an adopted document. The taskforce records its architecture decisions as architecture decision records (chapter 9, ADR-0001).
Open points
  • The governance names who adopts policies, minimum requirements and data models, but no body that adopts the system architecture as a whole. How the taskforce takes and confirms its decisions is not decided yet either. See chapter 11.
  • The accreditation of 1+MG IT infrastructure providers is still to be defined, and so is how it relates to audits and certification. See chapter 11.
  • Who informs data subjects after an access decision: the 1+MG NCP, or another national body DG VII.5.3. See chapter 11.
  • The 1+MG paths need an operational Genome EDIC. Until then, only externally governed datasets can be accessed through the system (ADR-0002, proposed). See chapter 11.
