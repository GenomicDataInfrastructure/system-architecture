8.1 Data protection by design and by default

In short
  • Every part of the system is built to protect the data from the start, and to use as little personal data as possible unless more is needed and approved. This is a legal duty of each controller under the GDPR (Art. 25), and a principle of the governance.
  • The main defaults: Users analyse personal data only in secure processing environments and can download only non-personal results; direct identifiers are replaced by key-coded identifiers, and genomic data are kept apart from clinical data; searches return counts, not records; Users get only the data their agreement covers, and only for as long as they need them.
  • The system gives the controllers the inputs for their data protection impact assessments: descriptions of the processing, the roles, the safeguards and the logs.

The problem​

The system processes genomic and health data of many people, across countries, for many Users. Genomic data can identify a person, and say something about their relatives. The data themselves stay federated, but some services see every country: the central services that register Users and manage access requests, and subject-level discovery, which searches every country's records. A mistake there affects data subjects in every country.

Data protection can't be added at the end. The GDPR requires each controller to build it into the means of processing, and to make the most protective option the default (Art. 25) [GDPR]. The European Data Protection Board (EDPB) explains that this applies throughout the lifecycle of the processing, and that the measures must be effective, not only present [EDPB Guidelines 4/2019]. The governance asks that the infrastructure be intrinsically compliant with the GDPR, building on data protection by design and by default DG II.2.

The rule​

  1. Every component must have privacy-protective defaults. Without a specific approval, it processes no personal data, or the least that its function needs, and gives no access.
  2. Users analyse personal data only in a secure processing environment (SPE), and can download only non-personal results DG II.2 DG VIII.2.2. The governance provides for a few other exits, each controlled and logged: reports of incidental findings to the entity mandated nationally DG VIII.11.2, temporary pooling in a certified SPE DG VIII.3.4, and, where applicable, the SPE of the European Commission DG VIII.2.2 (3.1).
  3. Every component must apply the technical and organisational measures (TOMs) that the Genome EDIC Assembly of Members adopts for all actors DG VI.1.1, and that reflect the high risk DG V.1.3.
  4. Every processing operation must have a named controller. Data protection by design is that controller's duty. 1+MG IT infrastructure providers build the measures into their platforms and support the controllers, including in their data protection impact assessments (DPIAs) DG V.1.3. Who is controller for each operation is in 8.2.
  5. Every component should produce the evidence of its measures: logs, configuration and documentation, for audits and DPIAs DG V.1.3.

How the measures are chosen and reviewed​

GDPR Art. 25(1) asks controllers to choose measures in the light of the state of the art, the cost, and the nature, scope, context and purposes of the processing, and to keep them effective over time [EDPB Guidelines 4/2019]. In the system:

  • the risk and quality management framework that the Assembly of Members adopts sets the method DG V.1.1; until it does, B1MG+ has proposed one [B1MG+ D2.9];
  • the Genome EDIC CC turns it into minimum requirements and audit criteria DG V.1.2;
  • each DPIA, and its regular updates, checks the measures against the risk and the state of the art DG VII.5.4;
  • external audits, and certification where it applies, check that the measures work DG V.1.3;
  • chapter 10 will give the indicators.

Data subjects' rights by design​

Data protection by design includes protecting the rights of data subjects (GDPR Art. 25(1)), and the governance makes it easy for data subjects to exercise them DG II.2 DG V.2.4. The essentials, detailed in 6.4 and 8.9:

  • One place to go: a national information portal, built on central tools and interfaces, where data subjects are informed, set their preferences and exercise their rights DG V.2.2 DG V.2.4. The 1+MG NCP is the contact point for those who don't use the portal DG V.2.5.
  • Requests reach the data: the national link between identity and key-coded identifier lets a request reach the right records DG V.2.4.
  • Changes reach the Users: dataset versioning, and the stable-dataset rule above DG V.2.6 DG V.2.7.
  • Limits are told in advance, before data subjects consent or object DG V.2.2, and only within what the GDPR and national law allow DG V.2.1.

See also 5.2.4.

Support for data protection impact assessments​

A DPIA is always the duty of the controller responsible for the processing DG VII.2.2. The governance requires these DPIAs and, as its own policy, a prior consultation of the supervisory authority under the GDPR (the data protection authority) for each. The GDPR itself requires the consultation only where the DPIA shows a high residual risk (Art. 36(1)).

The list is not exhaustive. Every controller, including the User Organisation for its project in the SPE, assesses under GDPR Art. 35 whether it needs a DPIA. The inputs below serve those DPIAs too, above all the SPE audit reports DG VIII.2.1.

The architecture gives each controller the inputs it needs:

  • A description of the processing: the lifecycle scenarios in chapter 6, and the requirements.
  • The roles: the controller, joint controllers and processors of each operation, in 8.2.
  • The data flows and where data are stored: the building blocks in chapter 5, and the deployment in chapter 7.
  • The safeguards: this page, and 8.3 to 8.8.
  • Evidence: audit reports of the SPEs, which the Genome EDIC CC makes available to User Organisations DG VIII.2.1, and the logs of 8.5.
  • A common risk method: the risk management framework that B1MG+ proposes for the Genome EDIC [B1MG+ D2.9], until the Assembly of Members adopts one DG V.1.1.

1+MG IT infrastructure providers must support the controllers in their DPIAs DG V.1.3.

Where it applies​

Everywhere. Each building block in chapter 5 lists the personal data it handles, and links back here. Legal and governance basis​

  • GDPR: principles (Art. 5), data protection by design and by default (Art. 25), security (Art. 32), DPIA and prior consultation (Art. 35 and 36), safeguards for research (Art. 89) [GDPR]. The EDPB guidelines on Art. 25 give its authoritative reading [EDPB Guidelines 4/2019].
  • Governance: the principle GDPR-compliant DG II.2; the Genome EDIC CC drives tools built for data protection by design and by default DG V.1.2; the TOMs the Assembly of Members adopts DG VI.1.1; the TOMs of the IT infrastructure providers DG V.1.3; the 1+MG Data Host infrastructure fulfils data protection by design and by default DG VI.4.6; the yearly reconfirmation and sleeping period follow the principle "that data should be exposed as little as possible" DG VIII.3.1.
  • Earlier 1+MG work: the 1+MG requirements for IT infrastructure were derived stage by stage from a data protection by design and by default approach [1+MG DPbDD IT requirements]. Because they pre-date the 2025-12 governance, we use them for the IT requirements, and take the roles from the governance.
  • EHDS, for comparison: SPEs used under the EHDS keep access logs for at least one year, and only non-personal data can be downloaded from them (EHDS Art. 73) [EHDS].
