8.2 Controllers, processors and responsibilities

In short
  • Each phase of the data lifecycle has its own controller. The 1+MG Data Provider is controller for including the data; the 1+MG Data Holder (1+MG compliant datasets) or the Genome EDIC (1+MG cohort datasets) for holding and disclosing them; the User Organisation for using them.
  • The Genome EDIC is processor for the User Organisation in the secure processing environments, and the SPE providers are its sub-processors. A 1+MG Data Host that is not the 1+MG Data Holder is the Data Holder's processor. Each of these roles is set out in an agreement.
  • Two questions are still open: whether the Genome EDIC is joint controller with the 1+MG Data Holder, and which role the SPE providers have for their own platform. Two architecture decision records propose answers; both are still to be confirmed.

The problem​

Many organisations in many countries process the same data, each for a different step. The GDPR needs one answer for every processing operation: who decides why and how it happens (the controller), and who only carries it out on the controller's behalf (the processor) (Art. 4(7), 4(8)) [GDPR]. Without that answer, nobody can be held accountable, the right agreements can't be signed, and data subjects don't know whom to ask.

This page is the one place that allocates the roles. Other pages link here. The Genome EDIC is the legal entity that holds its roles; it acts through its central office, the Genome EDIC CC.

The rule​

  1. Every processing operation must have a named controller, with its own legal basis. The governance gives each phase of the lifecycle its own controller or controllers DG II.1.
  2. GDPR roles follow the 1+MG role an organisation plays, not the organisation itself. One organisation may hold several roles DG III: a 1+MG Data Provider may also be the 1+MG Data Holder and the 1+MG Data Host. It then has the GDPR role of each, for each operation.
  3. The same actor can have different roles in different phases. The Genome EDIC is controller for the access decision on 1+MG cohort datasets, and processor for the User Organisation in the use phase.
  4. Every processor works under a contract or other legal act with its controller (GDPR Art. 28(3)), and engages sub-processors only with the controller's prior specific or general written authorisation (Art. 28(2)); for the federated SPEs, the governance asks the Genome EDIC to name its sub-processors DG VIII.2.1. Joint controllers set out their responsibilities in an arrangement, and make its essence available to data subjects (GDPR Art. 26).
  5. Each building block must say for which controller it processes, and log enough to show it, without logging more personal data than needed (8.1).

Each controller and processor can start its record of processing activities (GDPR Art. 30) from the rows in which it appears.

Two cases sit outside the table:

  • Externally governed datasets. Their controller for the disclosure is not set by the 1+MG data governance: it is the dataset's own controller, under its own rules DG VII.3.1. Once a User imports such data into a 1+MG SPE, the User Organisation is controller for their use, as for 1+MG data DG VIII.3.3.
  • Step 1 of ADR-0003, before the Genome EDIC is operational. Who is controller and processor for the central services and the SPEs then is not decided (chapter 11).
Open point

The GDPR roles of the 1+MG NCP for the check before inclusion and in the information chain, of the registry of User Organisations, of the central information components, of reaching back to data subjects, and of the identity link service are not assigned. See chapter 11.

Who notifies a personal data breach​

Under the GDPR, the controller notifies the supervisory authority under the GDPR (the data protection authority) without undue delay and, where feasible, within 72 hours, unless the breach is unlikely to result in a risk; and informs the data subjects where the risk is high. A processor informs its controller without undue delay (Art. 33 and 34) [GDPR].

The governance adds its own chain for misuse by a User. Users report data breaches to the Genome EDIC CC DG VIII.2.2. An SPE provider that suspects a User has breached purpose limitation or confidentiality revokes that User's access at once, and informs the Genome EDIC CC and, where applicable, the other affected SPE providers DG VIII.3.3. The Genome EDIC CC then formally withdraws the User's right of access under the data use agreement DG VIII.3.2. The governance suggests that it does so on behalf of all the 1+MG Data Holders, which depends on the open mandate question (chapter 11). The governance sets no chain for other security incidents; 6.5 covers them.

A breach in an SPE can also affect the controller for the disclosure, whose data the SPE holds. The governance's chain through the Genome EDIC CC lets it inform both. NIS2 reporting, where it applies, comes on top (2.1).

Where data subjects exercise their rights​

The controller of each phase answers for the rights of data subjects in that phase. Data subjects don't have to know which one it is:

  • they use one entry point: the national information portal, or the 1+MG NCP as contact point DG V.2.4 DG V.2.5;
  • the request goes to the controller concerned, through the national link between identity and key-coded identifier;
  • the Genome EDIC CC tells the Users whose data in use change DG V.2.2;
  • where there are joint controllers, their arrangement says who answers, and each of them can be asked (GDPR Art. 26(3)).

6.4 gives the steps.

Whether the Genome EDIC is joint controller with the 1+MG Data Holder​

For 1+MG compliant datasets, the governance names the 1+MG Data Holder as controller for the disclosure DG III, and mentions only conditionally that the Genome EDIC may be joint controller with it DG VII.2.2 DG VII.5.5. ADR-0009 sets out the options and proposes that the two are joint controllers for the disclosure, including subject-level discovery, with the 1+MG Data Holder alone taking the access decision. Whatever the answer, the system must record for each dataset who the controller or controllers for the disclosure are, and show it to Users and data subjects.

Open point

Whether the Genome EDIC is joint controller with the 1+MG Data Holder for 1+MG compliant datasets. See chapter 11.

The role of SPE providers​

The governance makes the SPE providers sub-processors of the Genome EDIC DG VIII.2.1, and also calls the 1+MG IT infrastructure providers "the controller for the technical and organisational measures to protect the data" DG VIII.3.3. ADR-0010 sets out the options and proposes that both hold, for different processing: sub-processor for the data the User analyses, and controller for the SPE provider's own platform processing, such as User accounts and security logs.

Open point

Whether 1+MG IT infrastructure providers are only sub-processors in the use phase, or also controllers for their own platform processing. See chapter 11.

Where it applies​

  • Building blocks: every building block in chapter 5 names its controller, and the processor that runs it.
  • Runtime: each step in chapter 6 names the controller of the processing it carries out.
  • Concepts: 8.1 (who performs which DPIA), 8.5 (whose logs), 8.6 (the legal basis of each controller), 8.12 (the controller per type of dataset).
  • Context: 3.3 (scopes are not controllers).

Legal and governance basis​

  • GDPR: controller and processor (Art. 4(7), 4(8)); responsibility of the controller (Art. 24); joint controllers (Art. 26); processors and sub-processors (Art. 28); records of processing (Art. 30); breach notification (Art. 33 and 34) [GDPR]. The guidelines of the European Data Protection Board (EDPB) on the concepts of controller and processor, on which the governance itself relies DG VII.2.2 [EDPB Guidelines 07/2020].
  • Governance: each phase with its own controller DG II.1; the definitions of the actors and types of dataset DG III; the agreements in the table above; the User Organisation as controller for the use DG VII.1.3; the Genome EDIC as processor, with sub-processors DG VIII.2.1.
  • EHDS: controllership in the EHDS (Art. 74); joint controllers and the Commission as processor in HealthData@EU and in the Commission's SPE (Art. 75(9), 75(10)). The responsibilities of controllers and processors in HealthData@EU will be set by the implementing act under Art. 75(12), still a draft [EHDS] [HDEU-IA draft].
  • Expert input: B1MG+, an EU-funded project that supports the 1+MG initiative, held an expert workshop on roles under the GDPR [B1MG+ MS17]. It informs the proposals in ADR-0009 and ADR-0010; it is not a decision of the Genome EDIC.
Open point

Until the Genome EDIC is operational, who is controller and processor for the central services and the SPEs is not decided. See chapter 11.
