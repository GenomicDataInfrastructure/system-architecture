1.1 Requirements overview

In short
  • The system must support the whole data lifecycle: bringing data in (inclusion), finding and requesting them (access), and analysing them in a secure processing environment (use).
  • Most requirements apply to all four types of use. Healthcare reuse has its own fast track, and some rules apply only to research, or to research and healthcare reuse. The type of dataset decides who reviews and decides on access.
  • The European Health Data Space adds requirements for the Genome EDIC only once the Genome EDIC joins HealthData@EU, the EU network for cross-border access to health data. Some 1+MG requirements go further, such as counting how many people match a search across countries.

Goals and the most important requirements​

The system serves the goals described in chapter 1: the data stay in the countries, Users see one system, every processing step has a controller, and data subjects are informed and their choices respected DG I DG II.1. Five requirements matter most, because the rest depend on them:

  1. One catalogue and one access procedure for all data, whatever the country or the type of dataset (REQ-AC-2, REQ-AC-4).
  2. Analysis only in a secure processing environment (SPE), with no download of personal data (REQ-US-1).
  3. Access only for the four types of use, each with its own form and review (REQ-AC-4, REQ-AC-5) DG II.3.
  4. The access decision follows the type of dataset: a 1+MG Data Holder decides on 1+MG compliant datasets, the Genome EDIC on 1+MG cohort datasets (REQ-AC-7).
  5. Common data models and metadata, so that data from many countries can be found and analysed together (REQ-IN-1).

The tables below list the requirements by phase of the lifecycle DG II.1. Each has an identifier, so that other pages can refer to it. They say what the system must do; chapters 4 to 8 say how. The column Type of use follows the governance, which marks rules that apply only to some types of use in the title of each section.

  • When 1+MG data may be processed in the SPE of the European Commission. See chapter 11.
  • Who decides on pooling for 1+MG compliant datasets. See chapter 11.

Two requirements apply across the phases:

  • Rights change the data. When data subjects exercise their rights, datasets are versioned, so that each User knows which version they use DG V.2.6.
  • Common security requirements. Every 1+MG IT infrastructure provider is audited by an external body against the minimum requirements of the Genome EDIC CC, and/or works towards certification; an SPE that pools data from other Member Countries must be certified DG V.1.3.

The system gives access only for the four types of use, and offers no route for screening the data for secondary findings, which the governance excludes DG II.4. It need not support the other things the governance leaves out: data not related to individuals, Users and members from third countries, and data from countries that are not members of the Genome EDIC. Chapter 1 explains each, and what the system still does for them.

What differs per type of dataset​

The type of dataset changes who reviews, who decides and who is controller, not what the User does. 8.12 Types of dataset describes each phase per type. In short:

  • 1+MG compliant datasets: the 1+MG Data Holder states with whom applications are shared DG VI.4.5, delegates the vetting of User Organisations DG VII.1.1, organises the review DG VII.4.6, decides, and performs the DPIA DG VII.5.4.
  • 1+MG cohort datasets: the Member Country decides who reviews, and a national veto is possible where applicable DG VII.4.7 (chapter 11); the Genome EDIC CC adopts and documents the decision DG VII.5.5.
  • Externally governed datasets: listed in the catalogue and importable into an SPE. No 1+MG access requirement applies to them: the User follows the dataset's own procedure DG VII.3.1 DG VIII.3.3.

Requirements from the EHDS​

The HealthData@EU requirements of the EHDS apply to the Genome EDIC only once it is an authorised participant in HealthData@EU (EHDS Art. 75(4)) [EHDS]. That is a target, not a status: the Commission connects an authorised participant by implementing decision (Art. 75(13)), under the conditions and compliance checks of the implementing act under Art. 75(12)(b), still a draft [HDEU-IA draft]. Separately, the EHDS applies to 1+MG Data Providers and 1+MG Data Holders that are health data holders in any case: its rules on secondary use apply from 26 March 2029, and for genomic data from 26 March 2031 (Art. 51(1)(f), 105). 2.1 gives the legal frame; 8.11 the design.

Some 1+MG requirements go beyond the EHDS. Subject-level data discovery has no equivalent in the EHDS DG VII.2.3. For SPEs, the EHDS asks health data access bodies for regular audits, including by third parties (EHDS Art. 73(3)); the governance asks every 1+MG IT infrastructure provider for an audit by an external body and/or work towards certification, and certification for an SPE that pools data from other Member Countries DG V.1.3.

Open points
  • The EHDS requirements for health data access applications are still under discussion, so the 1+MG form may have to change. See chapter 11.
  • The HealthData@EU implementing act is still a draft. See chapter 11.

Where the requirements come from​

The requirements summarise the governance [DG]. B1MG+, an EU-funded project that supports the 1+MG initiative (its outputs are expert input, not decisions), revisited the 1+MG use cases against the current governance, and shows who needs what for each [B1MG+ D2.6].

The GDI minimum viable product (MVP) already covers part of REQ-AC-2: the User Portal and the central dataset catalogue are in production, with access management for externally governed datasets. The 1+MG access request (REQ-AC-4) and the rest follow when the Genome EDIC is operational (ADR-0003, proposed). Chapter 1 lists what GDI delivers.

Open point

Until the Genome EDIC is operational, only externally governed datasets can be accessed through the system. See chapter 11.
