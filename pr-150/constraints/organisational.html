2.3 Organisational constraints

In short
  • Each Genome EDIC Member Country must put in place a 1+MG NCP, access to accredited IT infrastructure, people with the right expertise, and tools to inform data subjects. It must also avoid conflicts of interest where one organisation holds several roles.
  • The Genome EDIC Assembly of Members decides for all countries: policies, data models, security measures and minimum requirements. Each Member Country decides how it organises itself nationally: who holds which role, who reviews, and whether data may be pooled.
  • Every IT infrastructure provider is audited by an external body or works towards certification. An audit checks a provider against the Genome EDIC's minimum requirements; a certification (such as ISO 27001) is issued by an independent certification body; accreditation, which allows a provider to serve the Genome EDIC, is still to be defined. SPEs that pool data from several countries must be certified.

What each Member Country must put in place​

The governance makes each country's government responsible for having the national structures, people and tools in place DG I. The table lists what a Genome EDIC Member Country must provide, and what that means for the design. Each country records how it meets these constraints in its national implementation profile (template).

Who decides what​

The Genome EDIC Assembly of Members is the governing level, and decides on the principles and the minimum requirements DG I. Committees and working groups turn the principles into technical requirements DG II.1. Each Member Country decides how it implements the system nationally DG I.

The Genome EDIC CC prepares the decisions of the Assembly of Members, and turns its policies into minimum requirements for audit and certification DG V.1.2. Which body adopts this system architecture is not settled.

Open point

The governance names no body that adopts the system architecture as a whole. See chapter 11.

For the design, this means that every node must produce the evidence an auditor needs: logs, configuration, procedures and test results (8.3, 8.5). B1MG+ has proposed a risk management framework that each entity of the federation adapts [B1MG+ D2.9], and acceptance criteria for central and local nodes [B1MG+ D2.10].

Open point

How 1+MG IT infrastructure providers are accredited, how accreditation relates to audit and certification, and whether working towards certification without an external audit is enough. See chapter 11.

Readiness and the transition from GDI​

A country can assess its readiness for genomic medicine with the B1MG maturity level model [B1MG D5.1]. It measures maturity, not the obligations above. The GDI project helps countries build their nodes, and ends in March 2027 [GDI D4.4]. Who runs the central services if the Genome EDIC is not operational by then is not decided.

Risk

GDI may end before the Genome EDIC is operational. See chapter 11.
