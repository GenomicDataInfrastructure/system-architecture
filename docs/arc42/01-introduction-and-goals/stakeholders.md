<InShort>

- The main stakeholders are the actors of the 1+MG Data Governance, the rules under which the countries of the 1+ Million Genomes initiative (1+MG) share genomic data. They range from the Genome EDIC Assembly of Members to Users and data subjects. Each expects something different from the architecture, because each has different duties or rights. The public, the European Commission, auditors and the GDI project (the Genomic Data Infrastructure project, which builds the first version of the system) are affected too.
- This documentation has six kinds of readers: policy makers, legal experts, specialists in ethical, legal and societal implications (ELSI), security advisors, data protection officers and implementers. Each has a reader guide.
- The Genome EDIC Assembly of Members decides on the principles, policies and minimum requirements. Which body adopts this architecture as a whole is not decided yet.

</InShort>

## The stakeholders and what they expect

The [Genome EDIC](/glossary#genome-edic) is a European Digital Infrastructure Consortium: a legal entity, set up by a Commission implementing decision, that runs the 1+MG infrastructure <GovRef id="III" />. The actors and their names come from the governance definitions <GovRef id="III" />.

The system serves four types of use only: [research](/glossary#research), [policy development](/glossary#policy-development), [quality management in healthcare](/glossary#quality-management-in-healthcare) and [healthcare reuse](/glossary#healthcare-reuse) <GovRef id="II.3" />. Some expectations differ per type of use. The governance also has three types of dataset; the type decides who takes the access decision ([8.12](/concepts/dataset-types)).

This table covers what each actor expects from the architecture; [the next section](#readers-of-this-documentation) covers what readers need from its documentation. The expectations follow from each actor's duties in the governance, or, for data subjects, from their rights. The last column links to the pages that answer them; many of those pages are still being written. [3.3](/context/scopes) says which scope each actor belongs to, and [3.1](/context/business) what each exchanges with the system.

**How to reach these groups:** in the European scope, through the Genome EDIC CC, the one-stop shop for Users. To reach the taskforce that writes this documentation, open an issue in its [GitHub repository](https://github.com/GenomicDataInfrastructure/system-architecture/issues).

## Who decides on the architecture

- **The Genome EDIC Assembly of Members** decides on the principles and sets the minimum requirements <GovRef id="I" />. It adopts the policies <GovRef id="V.1.1" />, and the data models and technical and organisational measures <GovRef id="VI.1.1" />. The Genome EDIC CC prepares its decisions, with advice from committees and advisory boards <GovRef id="V.1.2" />.
- **Committees and working groups** turn the principles into implementation rules and technical requirements <GovRef id="II.1" />. For example, the Genome EDIC CC coordinates the definition of data models and ontologies through the Genome EDIC committees <GovRef id="VI.1.2" />.
- **Each Member Country** decides how it implements the system nationally, within the common requirements <GovRef id="II.2" />. It records its choices in its national implementation profile.
- **This documentation** is a working draft of the Genome EDIC system architecture taskforce, not an adopted document. The taskforce records its architecture decisions as architecture decision records ([chapter 9](/decisions), [ADR-0001](/decisions/0001-record-architecture-decisions)).

:::caution[Open points]
- The governance names who adopts policies, minimum requirements and data models, but no body that adopts the system architecture as a whole. How the taskforce takes and confirms its decisions is not decided yet either. See [chapter 11](/risks#architecture-adoption).
- The accreditation of 1+MG IT infrastructure providers is still to be defined, and so is how it relates to audits and certification. See [chapter 11](/risks#accreditation).
- Who informs data subjects after an access decision: the 1+MG NCP, or another national body <GovRef id="VII.5.3" />. See [chapter 11](/risks#national-information-flow).
- Who signs the data use agreement for 1+MG compliant datasets: whether the Genome EDIC is mandated to sign on behalf of the 1+MG Data Holders <GovRef id="VII.5.5" />. See [chapter 11](/risks#data-use-agreement-signatory).
- Whether the Genome EDIC is joint controller with the 1+MG Data Holder for 1+MG compliant datasets. See [chapter 11](/risks#joint-controllership).
- Access to 1+MG compliant and 1+MG cohort datasets needs an operational Genome EDIC. Until then, only externally governed datasets can be accessed through the system ([ADR-0003](/decisions/0003-implementation-in-steps), proposed). See [chapter 11](/risks#genome-edic-operational).
:::
