<InShort>

- The countries of the 1+ Million Genomes initiative (1+MG) share genomic data under the 1+MG Data Governance. The main stakeholders are the actors of that governance. They range from the Genome EDIC Assembly of Members to Users and data subjects. Each expects something different from the architecture, because each has different roles, responsibilities and rights. The public, the European Commission, auditors and the GDI project (the Genomic Data Infrastructure project, which builds the first version of the system) are affected too.
- This documentation has six kinds of readers: policy makers, legal experts, specialists in ethical, legal and societal implications (ELSI), security advisors, data protection officers and implementers. Each has a reader guide.
- The Genome EDIC Assembly of Members decides on the principles for the operations, and sets the minimum requirements and the rules for making genomic and related health data available for secondary use. Which body adopts this architecture as a whole is not decided yet.

</InShort>

## The stakeholders and what they expect

The [Genome EDIC](/glossary#genome-edic) is a European Digital Infrastructure Consortium: a legal entity, set up by a Commission implementing decision, that runs the 1+MG infrastructure <GovRef id="III" />. The stakeholders and their names come from the governance definitions <GovRef id="III" />.

The governance allows four types of use: [research](/glossary#research), [policy development](/glossary#policy-development), [quality management in healthcare](/glossary#quality-management-in-healthcare) and [healthcare reuse](/glossary#healthcare-reuse) <GovRef id="II.3" />. Some expectations differ per type of use. The governance also has three types of dataset: [1+MG compliant datasets](/glossary#1mg-compliant-datasets), [1+MG cohort datasets](/glossary#1mg-cohort-datasets) and [externally governed datasets](/glossary#externally-governed-datasets) <GovRef id="III" />. Who takes the access decision depends on the type ([chapter 8](/concepts)).

This table covers what each actor expects from the architecture; [the next section](#readers-of-this-documentation) covers what readers need from its documentation. The expectations follow from each stakeholder's responsibilities in the governance, or, for data subjects, from their rights. [Chapter 3](/context) says which [responsible party](/glossary#responsible-party) each actor belongs to, and what each exchanges with the system.

| Stakeholder | Role in the governance | What the stakeholder expects from the architecture |
|---|---|---|
| **[Genome EDIC Assembly of Members](/glossary#genome-edic-assembly-of-members)** (Genome EDIC) | Decision-making body. It decides on the principles, and adopts the policies, the minimum requirements, the data models and the technical and organisational measures <GovRef id="I" /> <GovRef id="V.1.1" /> <GovRef id="VI.1.1" />. | Its policies and minimum requirements can be enforced and checked in every Member Country, technically and through audits. Changes to rules and models can be rolled out. |
| **[Genome EDIC Central Coordination (Genome EDIC CC)](/glossary#genome-edic-central-coordination-genome-edic-cc)** (Genome EDIC) | Operational level. One-stop shop for [Users](/glossary#user), runs the central services. It sets the minimum requirements for audits and certification, which check that the policies of the Assembly of Members are followed. The national entities must meet them. It applies the same policies to its own work, with external audit or certification where applicable <GovRef id="I" /> <GovRef id="V.1.2" /> <GovRef id="VII.3.1" />. | Central services it can run efficiently: User Portal, catalogue, access request management, [User Organisation](/glossary#user-organisation) registry. A framework to audit and certify national entities. Tools built for data protection by design and by default. A channel to receive reports of data breaches, and a way to revoke access legally across all SPE providers <GovRef id="VIII.3.2" />. |
| **[1+MG DAC](/glossary#1mg-dac)** (Genome EDIC) | Reviews access requests, integrates the national opinions and vetoes, and seeks consensus <GovRef id="VII.5.1" />. It is provided by the Genome EDIC CC and gives an opinion, but doesn't decide: the 1+MG Data Holder decides on 1+MG compliant datasets, and the Genome EDIC CC on 1+MG cohort datasets, for which the Genome EDIC is controller <GovRef id="VII.5.4" /> <GovRef id="VII.5.5" />. | All the information on a request in one place, with the national opinions, the deadlines and a record of the outcome. A workflow that takes each request through the review steps, in the right order and on time. |
| **[Genome EDIC Member Country](/glossary#genome-edic-member-country)** | Puts the national structures and resources in place, and manages conflicts of interest. Decides the national strategy for the responsibility of downstream data disclosure, and nominates, where applicable, its 1+MG Data Holders. Enables [data subjects](/glossary#data-subject)' rights <GovRef id="I" /> <GovRef id="V.1.4" /> <GovRef id="VI.1.3" /> <GovRef id="V.2.4" />. Its [Genome EDIC national entities](/glossary#genome-edic-national-entities), the organisations that contribute to the operations in the country, implement the audit and certification requirements <GovRef id="V.1.2" />. | Clear obligations: what it must put in place. Freedom to fit the system into its national infrastructure, and to choose its tools and organisations, within the common requirements <GovRef id="II.2" />. A template to describe its choices. |
| **[1+MG NCP](/glossary#1mg-national-coordination-point-1mg-ncp)** (Member Country) | Checks data before inclusion, and channels access requests and opinions between the national bodies and the 1+MG DAC. It also informs data subjects. A Member Country may give this last task to another national body; which body does it is open <GovRef id="VI.4.4" /> <GovRef id="VII.4.4" /> <GovRef id="VII.5.3" /> ([chapter 11](/risks#national-information-flow)). | A national node and catalogue. Workflows that route requests, opinions and reminders to the right national bodies. Tools to inform data subjects, where it has that task. |
| **[1+MG Data Holder](/glossary#1mg-data-holder)** (Member Country; [1+MG compliant datasets](/glossary#1mg-compliant-datasets)) | Decides on access, after its own review, and performs the data protection impact assessment (<Acronym id="DPIA" />). It is the [controller](/glossary#controller) for the disclosure; whether the Genome EDIC is joint controller with it is open <GovRef id="VII.5.4" /> ([chapter 11](/risks#joint-controllership)). Delegates the vetting of User Organisations to the Genome EDIC <GovRef id="VII.1.1" />. | For research, policy development and quality management in healthcare: decision support, with the opinion of the 1+MG DAC, and the deadline for communicating the outcome: 15 working days, or 25 where ethical questions need more review <GovRef id="VII.4.6" />. For healthcare reuse there is no access decision per request <GovRef id="VII.5.4" />: the Genome EDIC CC agrees with the 1+MG Data Holder who co-reviews each request, next to one member of the 1+MG DAC, within 1 to 3 working days <GovRef id="VII.4.3" />. Support for its DPIA. |
| **[Local DAC](/glossary#local-dac)** (Member Country) | Reviews access requests for the datasets it oversees, whether 1+MG compliant or 1+MG cohort. For [1+MG cohort datasets](/glossary#1mg-cohort-datasets), it may send a veto on the opinion of the 1+MG DAC, with a justification <GovRef id="VII.4.7" />. When a national veto applies is open: see [chapter 11](/risks#national-veto). | Each request and the opinion of the 1+MG DAC in time, and, for 1+MG cohort datasets, a way to agree or send a veto within 10 working days. |
| **[1+MG Data Provider](/glossary#1mg-data-provider)** (Member Country) | Includes its data: transforms them into the agreed models, documents them, and informs data subjects before inclusion <GovRef id="VI.2.2" /> <GovRef id="VI.3.2" /> <GovRef id="V.2.3" />. It is the controller for making the data available, and performs a DPIA for the inclusion, with prior consultation of the data protection authority <GovRef id="VI.1.5" />. | Data models, instructions and tools for transformation and documentation. Clear information to give to data subjects. Support for its DPIA. A way to record data subjects' consent preferences and objections as <Acronym id="ELSI" /> metadata <GovRef id="VI.1.5" />. |
| **[1+MG Data Host](/glossary#1mg-data-host)** (Member Country) and **[1+MG IT infrastructure provider](/glossary#1mg-it-infrastructure-provider)** (a role, for the Genome EDIC or a Member Country) | The Data Host stores the data. Where it is not the 1+MG Data Holder, it is a processor under a data processing agreement <GovRef id="VI.4.6" />. IT infrastructure providers run the platforms and secure processing environments (<Acronym id="SPE">SPEs</Acronym>), centrally for the Genome EDIC or in a Member Country. Each IT infrastructure provider must be audited by an external body against the minimum requirements of the Genome EDIC CC, and/or work towards certification (ISO 27001, NIST or similar). Certification is required for an SPE that pools data from other Member Countries <GovRef id="V.1.3" /> <GovRef id="VIII.3.3" />. How 1+MG IT infrastructure providers are accredited is still to be defined <GovRef id="III" /> ([chapter 11](/risks#accreditation)). Where a provider falls under the NIS2 Directive, its risk-management and reporting obligations apply too <Cite id="nis2" />. | Common minimum requirements for platforms and SPEs. The [data use agreement](/glossary#data-use-agreement) as the basis for giving access, and a way to revoke access at once when a breach is suspected <GovRef id="VIII.3.3" />. |
| **[User Organisation](/glossary#user-organisation)** | Registers, names its Users and keeps that information up to date (for healthcare reuse, so that it can be checked in real time) <GovRef id="VII.1.3" />. Signs the terms of use and the data processing agreement with the Genome EDIC CC <GovRef id="VII.1.3" /> <GovRef id="VIII.2.1" />. The Genome EDIC CC issues the data use agreement for each approved request <GovRef id="VII.5.5" />; who signs it for the 1+MG Data Holders, for 1+MG compliant datasets, is open ([chapter 11](/risks#data-use-agreement-signatory)). The User Organisation is controller for what its Users do with the data, and joint controller for subject-level data discovery <GovRef id="VII.1.3" />. | One registration for all data in the Genome EDIC. A simple way to add and remove Users. Clear agreements that say what it is responsible for, as controller. |
| **[User](/glossary#user)** (User Organisation) | Finds data, requests access, and analyses the data in an SPE <GovRef id="VII.2.1" /> <GovRef id="VII.3.1" /> <GovRef id="VIII.3.1" />. Must not try to identify data subjects, link records without permission or export personal data, and reports errors in the data and data breaches to the Genome EDIC CC <GovRef id="VIII.2.2" />. For healthcare reuse, the User is a licensed healthcare professional, whose status is checked <GovRef id="VII.4.3" /> <GovRef id="VII.1.4" />; how it is checked is open ([chapter 11](/risks#healthcare-professional-check)). | One catalogue and one access procedure for all data in the Genome EDIC. SPEs with suitable tools and enough computing power <GovRef id="VIII.3.3" />, and the option to pause access ([sleeping period](/glossary#sleeping-period)) <GovRef id="VIII.3.1" /> <GovRef id="VIII.3.2" />. For healthcare reuse: a review within 1 to 3 working days <GovRef id="VII.4.3" />, and a way to put questions to the treating healthcare professional without learning who the professional or the patient is <GovRef id="VIII.4.1" /> <GovRef id="VIII.5.3" />. |
| **[Data subjects](/glossary#data-subject)** (other actor: they have rights, not responsibilities, in the system, and its rules exist to protect them <GovRef id="VII.5.1" />) | The people the data are about <GovRef id="III" />. | Information on how their data are used, in their language. To know who is responsible for their data, and whom to ask. To be told, before they consent or object, about limits to their rights in a project <GovRef id="V.2.2" />. The chance to consent or object where that applies. A way to exercise their rights, ideally through a portal in their Member Country <GovRef id="V.2.4" /> <GovRef id="VII.5.3" />. The results of projects that used their data <GovRef id="VIII.1.2" />. No direct contact from Users <GovRef id="VIII.5.3" />. |
| **The public** (other actor) | Is informed about how the data are used. Its contact in each country is the 1+MG NCP <GovRef id="III" />. | A register of authorised projects, statistics on access requests and decisions, and the results of data use, in the language of the country <GovRef id="VIII.1.2" /> <GovRef id="VIII.1.3" /> <GovRef id="VII.5.5" />. |

Data subjects may already be deceased <GovRef id="III" />, and they include minors and persons not able to consent <Cite id="b1mg-special-subjects" />. Genomic data also say something about a person's biological relatives. [Chapter 8](/concepts) says how the system takes this into account; the 1+MG guidance on transparency and consent is a starting point <Cite id="1mg-consent-guidance" />.

**How to reach these groups:** for the Genome EDIC, through the Genome EDIC CC, the one-stop shop for Users. In each country, through its 1+MG NCP, the main contact for the Genome EDIC and for national stakeholders, including the public <GovRef id="I" /> <GovRef id="III" />. To reach the taskforce that writes this documentation, open an issue in its [GitHub repository](https://github.com/GenomicDataInfrastructure/system-architecture/issues).

## Who decides on the architecture

- **The Genome EDIC Assembly of Members** decides on the principles and sets the minimum requirements <GovRef id="I" />. It adopts the policies <GovRef id="V.1.1" />, and the data models and technical and organisational measures <GovRef id="VI.1.1" />. The Genome EDIC CC prepares its decisions, with advice from committees and advisory boards <GovRef id="V.1.2" />.
- **Committees and working groups** turn the principles into implementation rules and technical requirements <GovRef id="II.1" />. For example, the Genome EDIC CC coordinates the definition of data models and ontologies through the Genome EDIC committees <GovRef id="VI.1.2" />.
- **Each Member Country** decides how it implements the system nationally, within the common requirements <GovRef id="II.2" />. It records its choices in its national implementation profile.
- **This documentation** is a working draft of the Genome EDIC system architecture taskforce, not an adopted document. The taskforce records its architecture decisions in [chapter 9](/decisions).

:::caution[Open points]
- The governance names who adopts policies, minimum requirements and data models, but no body that adopts the system architecture as a whole. How the taskforce takes and confirms its decisions is not decided yet either. See [chapter 11](/risks#architecture-adoption).
- Who informs data subjects after an access decision: the 1+MG NCP, or another national body <GovRef id="VII.5.3" />. See [chapter 11](/risks#national-information-flow).
- Whether the Genome EDIC is joint controller with the 1+MG Data Holder for 1+MG compliant datasets. See [chapter 11](/risks#joint-controllership).
- When a national veto applies to 1+MG cohort datasets: the governance gives Local DACs and 1+MG Data Providers a veto, but doesn't say in which cases it applies <GovRef id="VII.4.7" /> <GovRef id="VII.5.1" />. See [chapter 11](/risks#national-veto).
- The accreditation of 1+MG IT infrastructure providers is still to be defined, and so is how it relates to audits and certification. See [chapter 11](/risks#accreditation).
- Who signs the data use agreement for 1+MG compliant datasets: whether the Genome EDIC is mandated to sign on behalf of the 1+MG Data Holders <GovRef id="VII.5.5" />. See [chapter 11](/risks#data-use-agreement-signatory).
- How a User's status as a licensed healthcare professional is checked, for healthcare reuse <GovRef id="VII.4.3" />. See [chapter 11](/risks#healthcare-professional-check).
- Access to 1+MG compliant and 1+MG cohort datasets needs an operational Genome EDIC. Until then, only externally governed datasets can be accessed through the system. See [chapter 11](/risks#genome-edic-operational).
:::
