<InShort>

- Seen from outside, the Genome EDIC system is treated as a single unit: what the Genome EDIC and the Genome EDIC Member Countries run, and the bodies that run it or take decisions with it. Everyone else who exchanges information with it is a partner: Users and their organisations, data subjects, the systems of the 1+MG Data Providers, and European infrastructure such as HealthData@EU.
- Data about people come in mainly from the 1+MG Data Providers. Users work on them only inside secure processing environments, and take out only non-personal results. The few other ways in and out are listed on this page.
- A scope says who is responsible for what; it is not the same as being inside the system. The User Organisation, the 1+MG Data Providers (for their own systems) and treating healthcare professionals (the doctors of the patients concerned) take part in the framework, but stay outside the system.

</InShort>

## The system as a black box

This page looks at the [Genome EDIC system](/glossary#genome-edic-system) from outside, as a single unit. The system is what the [European scope](/glossary#european-scope) and the [national scope](/glossary#national-scope) run, and the bodies that run it or take decisions with it. It has a European part, run for the Genome EDIC, and a national part in each [Genome EDIC Member Country](/glossary#genome-edic-member-country). Together they let Users find data, apply for access and analyse the data in secure processing environments (<Acronym id="SPE">SPEs</Acronym>), and let data subjects be informed and exercise their rights. [Chapter 5](/building-blocks) shows what each part contains.

**Inside the boundary** are the bodies that run the system or take decisions with it: the [Genome EDIC CC](/glossary#genome-edic-central-coordination-genome-edic-cc), the [1+MG DAC](/glossary#1mg-dac), the [1+MG NCPs](/glossary#1mg-national-coordination-point-1mg-ncp), the [1+MG Data Holders](/glossary#1mg-data-holder), the [Local DACs](/glossary#local-dac), the [1+MG Data Hosts](/glossary#1mg-data-host), the [1+MG IT infrastructure providers](/glossary#1mg-it-infrastructure-provider) that run its platforms and SPEs, and other [Genome EDIC national entities](/glossary#genome-edic-national-entities). Their exchanges are steps inside the system, which [chapter 6](/runtime) describes. The [Genome EDIC Assembly of Members](/glossary#genome-edic-assembly-of-members) adopts the rules, models and minimum requirements the system follows; it doesn't exchange data with it ([1.3](/introduction/stakeholders)).

**Outside the boundary** are the User Organisations and their Users, the source systems of the 1+MG Data Providers, treating healthcare professionals and data subjects, and the other partners in the table below. Not everyone in the two scopes runs the system: see [Scopes and the system boundary](#scopes-and-the-system-boundary). Every partner appears in the diagram and the table. [3.2 Technical context](/context/technical) gives the channels and standards of each exchange.

Inside the boundary, the European part and the national part of each Member Country are run by different organisations. Each is audited or certified on its own <GovRef id="V.1.3" />. The lines between them are [trust boundaries](/glossary#trust-boundary): every exchange across them is authenticated and encrypted. What each crossing requires is decided in [ADR-0006](/decisions/0006-trust-boundaries) (wave 3), and described in [8.3](/concepts/security); [chapter 5](/building-blocks) draws the boundaries in detail.

Treating healthcare professionals don't act as [Genome EDIC national entities](/glossary#genome-edic-national-entities), and their exchange with the User takes place outside the activities of the Genome EDIC Secondary Use Framework <GovRef id="VIII.5.3" />. They are reached through the 1+MG NCP, which sets up the communication chain with national actors <GovRef id="VIII.4.5" /> <GovRef id="VIII.5.2" />. The Member Country enables the vetting of healthcare professionals <GovRef id="VII.1.4" />.
2. The governance makes each Member Country enable the link between a data subject's identity and their [key-coded identifiers](/glossary#key-coded-identifier), and the authentication of data subjects <GovRef id="V.2.4" />. The Genome EDIC CC offers an identification management service as one tool <GovRef id="VII.5.5" />. So the link is held either in the national [data subject services](/building-blocks/national/data-subject-services), inside the system, or in a national service outside it. Each Member Country records which in its [national implementation profile](/glossary#national-implementation-profile). Across this boundary pass the identity data needed to authenticate the data subject and, where the link is held outside, the key-coded identifiers of that person. The interfaces are decided in [ADR-0005](/decisions/0005-identity-link-and-key-coded-identifiers) (wave 3), and described in [8.7](/concepts/identifiers-and-linkage).
3. Contact with data subjects is national as a rule. The exceptions: a Genome EDIC information portal where a country can't serve its data subjects <GovRef id="V.2.2" />, and the Genome EDIC CC communicating results to data subjects <GovRef id="VIII.1.2" />.
4. These providers don't act as 1+MG Data Providers for these datasets: a 1+MG Data Provider makes data available in accordance with the 1+MG data governance <GovRef id="III" />. Some sections of the governance still call them "1+MG Data Providers" that are not willing or able to follow the harmonised governance <GovRef id="VII.2.1" /> <GovRef id="VII.3.1" />. They may be in a Member Country or outside.

The system relies on the assertions of identity providers and the identity federation. The level of assurance it requires is decided in [ADR-0004](/decisions/0004-identity-and-authorisation) (wave 3), and described in [8.4](/concepts/identity-and-access).

:::caution[Risks]
**At the boundary:**

- All Users sign in through an identity federation, so its availability limits the availability of the system. See [chapter 11](/risks#identity-federation-dependency).
- Data and software imported into SPEs from HDABs, providers of externally governed datasets and Users can carry malicious code or data the User may not hold <GovRef id="VIII.3.1" />. See [chapter 11](/risks#spe-imports).
- The interfaces with HealthData@EU are still a draft, and may change. See [chapter 11](/risks#healthdata-eu-act).
- For healthcare reuse, the system must check a User's status in real time and support a review within 1 to 3 working days; how the status is checked is still open. See [chapter 11](/risks#healthcare-professional-check) and [chapter 11](/risks#healthcare-review-time).
:::

## Where data about people come in and go out

This section covers data about data subjects. The system also receives personal data about Users (identity, status), contacts in User Organisations, and data subjects who exercise their rights: see the table.

- **In:** from the systems of the 1+MG Data Providers <GovRef id="VI.2.2" />. Also, into a User's SPE only: data from HDABs, from providers of externally governed datasets, and the User's own data, after a security review <GovRef id="VIII.3.1" /> <GovRef id="VIII.3.3" />. From Users: enriched or improved data, where the transfer is lawful <GovRef id="VIII.9.1" />, and reports of incidental findings <GovRef id="VIII.11.1" />. For healthcare reuse, additional information from treating healthcare professionals, within their patient's consent <GovRef id="VIII.4.4" />. For data subjects who exercise their rights, identity data from national identity services <GovRef id="V.2.4" />.
- **Out, to Users:** only non-personal results of analysis <GovRef id="II.2" />, and the number of records that match a subject-level data discovery <GovRef id="VII.2.3" />. Small counts can reveal whether a person is in the data, so the discovery service applies the safeguards of its DPIA <GovRef id="VII.2.2" /> ([8.8](/concepts/output-control)).
- **Discovery:** a subject-level data discovery processes personal data inside the system, under the joint controllership of the User Organisation and the controller for the disclosure (the 1+MG Data Holder or the Genome EDIC, depending on the type of dataset) <GovRef id="VII.1.3" />. The 1+MG Data Host logs each search and watches for suspicious use <GovRef id="VII.2.3" />.
- **Out, elsewhere:**
  - to data subjects, information about the use of their own data <GovRef id="VII.5.3" />;
  - to the entity mandated nationally to inform data subjects of incidental findings, where the return is allowed <GovRef id="VIII.11.2" /> <GovRef id="VIII.11.3" />;
  - to treating healthcare professionals, Users' questions about a patient, routed so that the User never learns who the patient or the professional is <GovRef id="VIII.4.1" />;
  - to the SPE of the European Commission, where applicable <GovRef id="VIII.2.2" /> (open point).

How the data are protected on the way in and inside the system ([key-coded identifiers](/glossary#key-coded-identifier), SPEs, output control) is described in [8.7](/concepts/identifiers-and-linkage), [5.2.3](/building-blocks/national/spe) and [8.8](/concepts/output-control).

## Scopes and the system boundary

A scope says **who is responsible**. The system boundary says **what runs inside the Genome EDIC system**. They are not the same line ([3.3](/context/scopes)).

- **The User Organisation scope is outside the system.** The [User Organisation](/glossary#user-organisation) is [controller](/concepts/roles) for the downstream use of the data, that is, for what its Users do with the data in the SPE <GovRef id="VII.1.3" />, but its people and its IT stay outside. Its Users work on the data inside an SPE of the national scope, and take out only non-personal results <GovRef id="II.2" />. The User Organisation's IT touches the system in three places: its identity provider (through the identity federation), the devices its Users connect from (Users may not download personal data to them <GovRef id="II.2" />), and the data and software it imports into an SPE after a security review <GovRef id="VIII.3.1" />.
- **Some of the national scope is outside the system too.** The 1+MG Data Providers are responsible for including their data <GovRef id="VI.2.2" />, but their own systems, where the data come from, are outside. So are the registry of healthcare professionals, if a Member Country provides one <GovRef id="VII.4.3" />, and national identity services.
- **Treating healthcare professionals are outside every scope.** They are not Genome EDIC national entities <GovRef id="VIII.5.3" />, and are reached through the 1+MG NCP's communication chain <GovRef id="VIII.4.5" />.
- **Data subjects are outside every scope:** they have rights, not duties, in the system, and its rules exist to protect them <GovRef id="VII.5.1" />. Contact with them is a national task as a rule <GovRef id="I" />.

:::caution[Open points]
- **When 1+MG data may be processed in the SPE of the European Commission,** "where applicable" <GovRef id="VIII.2.2" />, and who would then be controller. The governance prefers federation to pooling in that SPE <GovRef id="V.1.3" />. See [chapter 11](/risks#commission-spe).
- **How a User's status as a healthcare professional is checked,** and so whether a national registry is a partner <GovRef id="VII.4.3" />. See [chapter 11](/risks#healthcare-professional-check).
- **Who informs data subjects** after an access decision: the 1+MG NCP, or another national body <GovRef id="VII.5.3" />. See [chapter 11](/risks#national-information-flow).
- **Whether data subjects are asked again** for healthcare reuse and recruitment into clinical trials <GovRef id="VII.5.3" />. See [chapter 11](/risks#repeated-consent).
- **Whether and how incidental findings reach data subjects:** the legal basis and the national communication chain are still open <GovRef id="VIII.11.1" /> <GovRef id="VIII.11.3" />. See [chapter 11](/risks#incidental-findings).
- **Whether enriched or improved personal data can be returned** to the 1+MG Data Providers: a legal basis is not established yet <GovRef id="VIII.9.1" />. See [chapter 11](/risks#enriched-data-return).
- **Who signs the data use agreement for 1+MG compliant datasets** <GovRef id="VII.5.5" />. See [chapter 11](/risks#data-use-agreement-signatory).
- **The exchanges with HealthData@EU** depend on the draft HealthData@EU implementing act, and on the EHDS requirements for access applications. See [chapter 11](/risks#healthdata-eu-act) and [chapter 11](/risks#ehds-access-applications).
- **Access to 1+MG compliant and 1+MG cohort datasets needs an operational Genome EDIC.** Until then, only externally governed datasets can be accessed through the system ([ADR-0003](/decisions/0003-implementation-in-steps), proposed). See [chapter 11](/risks#genome-edic-operational).
:::
