3.1 Business context

In short
  • Seen from outside, the Genome EDIC system is treated as a single unit: what the Genome EDIC and the Genome EDIC Member Countries run, and the bodies that run it or take decisions with it. Everyone else who exchanges information with it is a partner: Users and their organisations, data subjects, the systems of the 1+MG Data Providers, and European infrastructure such as HealthData@EU.
  • Data about people come in mainly from the 1+MG Data Providers. Users work on them only inside secure processing environments, and take out only non-personal results. The few other ways in and out are listed on this page.
  • A scope says who is responsible for what; it is not the same as being inside the system. The User Organisation, the 1+MG Data Providers (for their own systems) and treating healthcare professionals (the doctors of the patients concerned) take part in the framework, but stay outside the system.

The system as a black box​

This page looks at the Genome EDIC system from outside, as a single unit. The system is what the European scope and the national scope run, and the bodies that run it or take decisions with it. It has a European part, run for the Genome EDIC, and a national part in each Genome EDIC Member Country. Together they let Users find data, apply for access and analyse the data in secure processing environments (SPEs), and let data subjects be informed and exercise their rights. Chapter 5 shows what each part contains.

Inside the boundary are the bodies that run the system or take decisions with it: the Genome EDIC CC, the 1+MG DAC, the 1+MG NCPs, the 1+MG Data Holders, the Local DACs, the 1+MG Data Hosts, the 1+MG IT infrastructure providers that run its platforms and SPEs, and other Genome EDIC national entities. Their exchanges are steps inside the system, which chapter 6 describes. The Genome EDIC Assembly of Members adopts the rules, models and minimum requirements the system follows; it doesn't exchange data with it (1.3).

Outside the boundary are the User Organisations and their Users, the source systems of the 1+MG Data Providers, treating healthcare professionals and data subjects, and the other partners in the table below. Not everyone in the two scopes runs the system: see Scopes and the system boundary. Every partner appears in the diagram and the table. 3.2 Technical context gives the channels and standards of each exchange.

Inside the boundary, the European part and the national part of each Member Country are run by different organisations. Each is audited or certified on its own DG V.1.3. The lines between them are trust boundaries: every exchange across them is authenticated and encrypted. What each crossing requires is decided in ADR-0006 (wave 3), and described in 8.3; chapter 5 draws the boundaries in detail.

The diagram splits the system into its European and national parts, so that you can see which part handles each exchange. It shows each partner once, with the main data it sends and receives (select it to open it full size). Supervisory and audit bodies are drawn as one group. A dashed arrow depends on an open point or shows the target state. The table gives all exchanges.

What GDI already delivers. The GDI project delivers a first version of the system: a central catalogue, a User Portal as the one entry point, sign-in through an identity federation, and import of data into an SPE (ADR-0003, proposed) [GDI D3.6]. The 1+MG access requests and the connection to HealthData@EU are target state.

The system serves four types of use only: research, policy development, quality management in healthcare and healthcare reuse DG II.3. Healthcare reuse is the use of the data to support the care of a patient.

They may be in a Member Country or outside.

The system relies on the assertions of identity providers and the identity federation. The level of assurance it requires is decided in ADR-0004 (wave 3), and described in 8.4.

Risks

At the boundary:

  • All Users sign in through an identity federation, so its availability limits the availability of the system. See chapter 11.
  • Data and software imported into SPEs from HDABs, providers of externally governed datasets and Users can carry malicious code or data the User may not hold DG VIII.3.1. See chapter 11.
  • The interfaces with HealthData@EU are still a draft, and may change. See chapter 11.
  • For healthcare reuse, the system must check a User's status in real time and support a review within 1 to 3 working days; how the status is checked is still open. See chapter 11 and chapter 11.

Where data about people come in and go out​

This section covers data about data subjects. The system also receives personal data about Users (identity, status), contacts in User Organisations, and data subjects who exercise their rights: see the table.

  • In: from the systems of the 1+MG Data Providers DG VI.2.2. Also, into a User's SPE only: data from HDABs, from providers of externally governed datasets, and the User's own data, after a security review DG VIII.3.1 DG VIII.3.3. From Users: enriched or improved data, where the transfer is lawful DG VIII.9.1, and reports of incidental findings DG VIII.11.1. For healthcare reuse, additional information from treating healthcare professionals, within their patient's consent DG VIII.4.4. For data subjects who exercise their rights, identity data from national identity services DG V.2.4.
  • Out, to Users: only non-personal results of analysis DG II.2, and the number of records that match a subject-level data discovery DG VII.2.3. Small counts can reveal whether a person is in the data, so the discovery service applies the safeguards of its DPIA DG VII.2.2 (8.8).
  • Discovery: a subject-level data discovery processes personal data inside the system, under the joint controllership of the User Organisation and the controller for the disclosure (the 1+MG Data Holder or the Genome EDIC, depending on the type of dataset) DG VII.1.3. The 1+MG Data Host logs each search and watches for suspicious use DG VII.2.3.
  • Out, elsewhere:
    • to data subjects, information about the use of their own data DG VII.5.3;
    • to the entity mandated nationally to inform data subjects of incidental findings, where the return is allowed DG VIII.11.2 DG VIII.11.3;
    • to treating healthcare professionals, Users' questions about a patient, routed so that the User never learns who the patient or the professional is DG VIII.4.1;
    • to the SPE of the European Commission, where applicable DG VIII.2.2 (open point).

How the data are protected on the way in and inside the system (key-coded identifiers, SPEs, output control) is described in 8.7, 5.2.3 and 8.8.

Scopes and the system boundary​

A scope says who is responsible. The system boundary says what runs inside the Genome EDIC system. They are not the same line (3.3).

  • The User Organisation scope is outside the system. The User Organisation is controller for the downstream use of the data, that is, for what its Users do with the data in the SPE DG VII.1.3, but its people and its IT stay outside. Its Users work on the data inside an SPE of the national scope, and take out only non-personal results DG II.2. The User Organisation's IT touches the system in three places: its identity provider (through the identity federation), the devices its Users connect from (Users may not download personal data to them DG II.2), and the data and software it imports into an SPE after a security review DG VIII.3.1.
  • Some of the national scope is outside the system too. The 1+MG Data Providers are responsible for including their data DG VI.2.2, but their own systems, where the data come from, are outside. So are the registry of healthcare professionals, if a Member Country provides one DG VII.4.3, and national identity services.
  • Treating healthcare professionals are outside every scope. They are not Genome EDIC national entities DG VIII.5.3, and are reached through the 1+MG NCP's communication chain DG VIII.4.5.
  • Data subjects are outside every scope: they have rights, not duties, in the system, and its rules exist to protect them DG VII.5.1. Contact with them is a national task as a rule DG I.
Open points
  • When 1+MG data may be processed in the SPE of the European Commission, "where applicable" DG VIII.2.2, and who would then be controller. The governance prefers federation to pooling in that SPE DG V.1.3. See chapter 11.
  • How a User's status as a healthcare professional is checked, and so whether a national registry is a partner DG VII.4.3. See chapter 11.
  • Who informs data subjects after an access decision: the 1+MG NCP, or another national body DG VII.5.3. See chapter 11.
  • Whether data subjects are asked again for healthcare reuse and recruitment into clinical trials DG VII.5.3. See chapter 11.
  • Whether and how incidental findings reach data subjects: the legal basis and the national communication chain are still open DG VIII.11.1 DG VIII.11.3. See chapter 11.
  • Whether enriched or improved personal data can be returned to the 1+MG Data Providers: a legal basis is not established yet DG VIII.9.1. See chapter 11.
  • Who signs the data use agreement for 1+MG compliant datasets DG VII.5.5. See chapter 11.
  • The exchanges with HealthData@EU depend on the draft HealthData@EU implementing act, and on the EHDS requirements for access applications. See chapter 11 and chapter 11.
  • Access to 1+MG compliant and 1+MG cohort datasets needs an operational Genome EDIC. Until then, only externally governed datasets can be accessed through the system (ADR-0003, proposed). See chapter 11.
