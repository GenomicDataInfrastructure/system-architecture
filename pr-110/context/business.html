3.1 Business context

In short
  • Seen from outside, the Genome EDIC system is one black box: the IT that the Genome EDIC and the Genome EDIC Member Countries run. Everyone else who exchanges information with it is a partner: Users and their organisations, data subjects, the systems of the 1+MG Data Providers, and European infrastructure such as HealthData@EU.
  • Data about people come in mainly from the 1+MG Data Providers. Users work on them only inside secure processing environments, and take out only non-personal results. The few other ways in and out are listed on this page.
  • Belonging to a scope is not the same as being inside the system. The User Organisation, the systems of the 1+MG Data Providers and treating healthcare professionals have duties in the framework, but they stay outside the system.

The system as a black box​

This page looks at the Genome EDIC system from outside. The system is what the European scope and the national scope run: their services, and the bodies that work with them.

  • European services, run for the Genome EDIC: the 1+MG User Portal and central data catalogue, access request management, the User Organisation registry, transparency and reporting, and the connection to HealthData@EU (5.1).
  • National services, run in each Genome EDIC Member Country: the 1+MG NCP node and national catalogue, access review and decision support, secure processing environments (SPEs), data subject services, 1+MG compliant local IT infrastructure and data transformation (5.2).

The bodies that run these services or take decisions with them, such as the Genome EDIC CC, the 1+MG DAC and the 1+MG NCPs, are inside the boundary. Their exchanges are steps inside the system, which chapter 6 describes. Not everyone in the two scopes runs the system, though: see Scopes and the system boundary. Everyone and everything else that exchanges information with the system is a partner, and appears in the diagram and the table below. 3.2 Technical context gives the channels and standards of each exchange.

The diagram splits the system into its European and national parts, so that you can see which part handles each exchange. It shows each partner once, with its main exchanges (select it to open it full size). A dashed arrow depends on an open point. The table gives all exchanges.

Where data about people come in and go out​

  • In: from the systems of the 1+MG Data Providers DG VI.2.2. Also, into a User's SPE only: data from HDABs, from providers of externally governed datasets, and the User's own data, after a security review DG VIII.3.1 DG VIII.3.3. From Users: enriched or improved data DG VIII.9.1, and reports of incidental findings DG VIII.11.1. For healthcare reuse, additional information from treating healthcare professionals, within their patient's consent DG VIII.4.4.
  • Out, to Users: only non-personal results of analysis DG II.2, and the number of records that match a subject-level data discovery DG VII.2.3.
  • Discovery: a subject-level data discovery processes personal data inside the system, under the joint controllership of the User Organisation and the controller for the disclosure DG VII.1.3. The 1+MG Data Host logs each search and watches for suspicious use DG VII.2.3.
  • Out, elsewhere:
    • to data subjects, information about the use of their own data, and incidental findings where their return is allowed DG VII.5.3 DG VIII.11.1;
    • to treating healthcare professionals, Users' questions about a patient, routed so that the User never learns who the patient or the professional is DG VIII.4.1;
    • to the SPE of the European Commission, where applicable DG VIII.2.2 (open point).

How the data are protected on the way in and inside the system (key-coded identifiers, SPEs, output control) is described in 8.7, 5.2.3 and 8.8.

Scopes and the system boundary​

A scope says who is responsible. The system boundary says what runs inside the Genome EDIC system. They are not the same line (3.3).

  • The User Organisation scope is outside the system. The User Organisation is controller for the downstream use of the data, that is, for what its Users do with the data in the SPE DG VII.1.3, but its people and its IT stay outside. Its Users work on the data inside an SPE of the national scope, and take out only non-personal results DG II.2. The User Organisation's IT touches the system in three places: its identity provider, the devices its Users connect from (Users may not download personal data to them DG II.2), and the data and software it imports into an SPE after a security review DG VIII.3.1.
  • Some of the national scope is outside the system too. The 1+MG Data Providers are responsible for including their data DG VI.2.2, but their own systems, where the data come from, are outside. Treating healthcare professionals and the registry of healthcare professionals are reached through the Member Country, which provides the communication chain DG VIII.5.2, but they are not Genome EDIC national entities DG VIII.5.3 and not part of the system.
  • Data subjects are outside every scope. Contact with them is a national task DG I.
Open points
  • When 1+MG data may be processed in the SPE of the European Commission, "where applicable" DG VIII.2.2. The governance prefers federation to pooling in that SPE DG V.1.3. See chapter 11.
  • How a User's status as a healthcare professional is checked, and so whether a national registry is a partner DG VII.4.3. See chapter 11.
  • Who informs data subjects after an access decision: the 1+MG NCP, or another national body DG VII.5.3. See chapter 11.
  • Whether incidental findings can be returned to data subjects DG VIII.11.1. See chapter 11.
  • The exchanges with HealthData@EU depend on the draft HealthData@EU implementing act, and on the EHDS requirements for access applications. See chapter 11 and chapter 11.
  • The 1+MG paths need an operational Genome EDIC. Until then, only externally governed datasets can be accessed through the system (ADR-0002, proposed). See chapter 11.
